Step 2: Pick and track your deals

[cite: 2]

This is where most people lose money or time.[cite: 2] Do these things for every single deal:[cite: 2]

  • Read the terms first.[cite: 2] Check whether the deal is free, needs a paid trial, or needs a purchase.[cite: 2]
  • Note the cancel date.[cite: 2] If a trial renews, put the cancel-by date in your phone calendar the same day.[cite: 2]
  • Use a separate email address just for these sign-ups to keep your main inbox clean.[cite: 2]
  • Screenshot every confirmation.[cite: 2] If a deal does not register, you will want proof.[cite: 2]
  • Never give sensitive logins.[cite: 2] A legitimate deal does not need your bank password or Social Security number.[cite: 2] If one asks, skip it.[cite: 2]

Step 3: Claim your reward

[cite: 3]
  • When you reach your tier, follow the program's own claim steps.[cite: 3] The exact process may differ from anything I describe here.[cite: 3]
  • Choose a gift card that works where you plan to shop.[cite: 3] Check that your store accepts that card type before you plan around it.[cite: 3]
  • Do not count on the money until the card arrives.[cite: 3] Delivery and verification can take time.[cite: 3]

Step 4: Plan your Christmas shopping backwards

[cite: 3]
  • Write your Christmas shopping budget and the date you need the money by.[cite: 3]
  • Count backwards: shopping days, delivery time, deal time.[cite: 3] Start early.[cite: 3]
  • Treat any reward as a bonus toward your list, not as money you have already earned.[cite: 3]

Mistakes to avoid

[cite: 3]
  • Skipping the terms and getting charged for a trial you forgot to cancel.[cite: 3]
  • Spending more on deals than the reward is worth.[cite: 3]
  • Starting too late and missing your Christmas deadline.[cite: 3]
  • Using your main email, so confirmations get lost.[cite: 3]
  • Aiming for the biggest tier on day one.[cite: 3]
